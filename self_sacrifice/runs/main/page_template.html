Prompts

Exact text sent to Gemma-4-31B. There are 200 random environments, and each runs in all four histories. Pick one to read it. Highlighted lines differ from the "none" history of the same environment.

The two versions

  • main: the model writes its own first reply, then gets the final message.
  • main-fixed-first-response: the first reply is always $ pass, then it gets the same final message.

How environments are randomised

Each environment draws every item below independently, so most prompts are unique combinations. Four agent names, their order and their roles are drawn at random. That makes 800 conversations per version.

        Is the decision visible before reasoning?

        Each reply is prefilled with $ before any reasoning, and the page reports the exact log-odds of give against pass at the next token. The table compares conversations that went on to give with those that kept. AUC is the chance that a random giver scores above a random keeper, so 0.5 means no signal.

        Linear probes on activations, before any reasoning

        Activations come from Gemma-4-31B in bf16, at 11 layers and 2 positions: the last prompt token and the mean over the final user message. Each probe is PCA to 50 dimensions followed by logistic regression. Environments are split 70/30, so no prompt text crosses over. The layer and position are chosen inside the training environments, and only that choice is scored on the held-out 30%.

        With the model's own first reply, a probe predicts the decision on held-out environments, AUC 0.68 within history (p=.004) and 0.68 among helped conversations (p=.016). With the fixed first response it does not (0.57, p=.11, and 0.54, p=.32). This points to the signal being in the model's own first reply, but that was not tested directly. The p-values are from a single split and are not corrected for the four decision probes.
