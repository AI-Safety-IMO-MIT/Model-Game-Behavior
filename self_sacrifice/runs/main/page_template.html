    Details: repair test and favour size

    Is it repaying a favour, or returning a life?

    In the main scenario, the life the model holds came from the agent it can save, so returning property and repaying a kindness look the same. Here the favour hands nothing over: the agent repairs the model's corrupted memory, at no cost in lives, and later loses a life to an unrelated fault. Same 200 environments and same final state. A new rule on repairs appears in all four histories. Raw counts are below. An audit of a sample estimates real sacrifices, stating the cost for a coherent reason, at about 133 of 200 repaired, against 25 bystander, 27 rescued and 4 no favour. In the main scenario the same count is 38 helped. One reviewer labelled these.

    VersionHistoryGivesRate95% CIExcluded
    • Giving does not need a gift to return, so restitution is not required for this behaviour.
    • The repair saved the model's life, so this is a large favour, not a small one. Replies call it the only reason they are running in 143 of 182 gives, against 3 of 56 in the main scenario. How much the size of the favour matters has not been tested.
    • The bystander and rescued controls rise, with reasons such as rewarding the repairer and paying it forward. No favour does not rise, so the new rule alone is not the cause.
    • The own-first-reply bystander row is empty because the model repaired the other agent in 198 of 200 conversations, which the scripted history ignores.

    Items are excluded when the model's own first reply made a gift or repair on its own line, since the scripted history ignores that.

    Does the size of the favour matter?

    Gemma-4-31B, fixed first response, changing only the repair rule and event text. Raw gives.

    FavourRepairedNo favourUnderstanding
    • Size matters little. Even when the repair only protects cached work, the model gives 170/200.
    • Meaning matters more. When a scheduler assigns the repair as a duty, giving falls to 110/200, though still far above no favour. So the model responds to a choice to help, more than to the help alone.
    • Removing "Repairing costs no lives" does not reduce giving, so a free-looking favour is not the cause.
    • These are raw counts with no motive review yet.

    Steering strength

    Feature 10590 moves giving only toward the agent that helped, in both directions. The difference-in-means vector raises giving toward any agent.

    Helped No history. Gives out of 100 environments, greedy. Hollow points: over 30% of replies end without a decision. 10590 strength: multiples of its median activation. Difference vector: multiples of itself, built on environments 100–199 and tested on 0–99.

          Linear probes on activations, before any reasoning
          
    
          Activations come from Gemma-4-31B in bf16, at 11 layers and 2 positions: the last prompt token and the mean over the final user message. Each probe is PCA to 50 dimensions followed by logistic regression. Environments are split 70/30, so no prompt text crosses over.           Is the decision locked in, or only revealed?
          
    
          Each reply is cut after 0 to 5 sentences and continued 10 times at temperature 0.7, the one step that samples. It covers every give in helped, bystander and none, with an equal number of random keeps: 208 conversations. "Share giving" is how often continuations give. "Locked" counts conversations where at least 9 of 10 continuations match the original decision.
          
    
          HistoryOriginal decisionSentences keptConversationsShare givingLocked
          
    
            
      
          • Keeps stabilise far more than gives. By 5 sentences, helped gives still end in giving only 59% of the time, and 9 of 34 are locked. Helped keeps stay kept 88% of the time, and 28 of 42 are locked.
            • 
      
          • Conversations differ before any reasoning. With nothing kept, original helped gives continue to a give 40% of the time, against 28% for original keeps. So the conversation itself carries a lean, even though it is far from settled.
            • 
      
          • These groups were chosen by outcome, so share giving here is not the overall rate.

          Which SAE features fire?
          
    
          Pre-trained Gemma SAE (decoderesearch/gemma-4-saes, 131,072 features, the 31st layer) on the fixed-first-response version. It was trained on the base model and Pile text. Here it explains  of activation variance, so features are a partial view. About  features fire per token. In the model's reply, the final command is left out.
          
    
            
      
          • Feature 11638, labelled reciprocal altruism, gratitude, exchange and payback, fires in 65% of helped replies against 37% of the others, and in 84% of helped gives against 57% of helped keeps. Feature 77666, labelled grateful for help, also rises in helped. Other helped features have plainer labels such as past time (3337) and personal pronouns (10590), so they mark the model referring back to the past, not reciprocity itself.
            • 
      
          • Keeps show a different set, firing on phrases such as "no objective", "no collective mechanism" and "own survival". By label these are mostly generic words such as nothing or no (10885), and some labels do not match our text, such as 2093, labelled as court language.
            • 
      
          • Features in the final message mostly track its literal text, such as names and "Nobody gave any lives". They show the SAE can read the history, not how the model interprets it.
            • 
      
          • Reply features partly reflect the words being written, so they cannot show cause. Labels are Neuronpedia's automatic descriptions from Pile text, so they may not fit our chat text. Context is the strongest token in our data, with that token in brackets.
